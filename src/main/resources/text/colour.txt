This is one of the most easily recognisable characteristics of a wine. Wine colour can be split into three different categories; red, white, and rose. Red wines are deep red (hence the name). White wines range in physical colour, some have a greenish tinge whereas others are a bright yellow-golden colour. Rosé wines are a light pink in colour. Each of these three wine colours looks different because of the amount of tannins allowed to seep into the wine from the grape skin during fermentation. Tannins are substances that naturally occur in grapes. they are often described as bitter tasting meaning the more tannins in the grape, the bitterer the wine.

Red Wines -
Red wines are made from red grapes, whose skins have more tannins than white grape skins which is why red wines tend to be more bitter tasting than white wines.

Rosé wines -
Rosé wines are pink in colour as they stay in contact with red grape skins for a relatively short time compared to red wine. Though colourful, Rosé wines tend to taste more similar to white wines than red wines as they have relatively low tannin.

White Wines -
While white wines do still include tannin, there is not enough of it to make it very bitter tasting. Instead, white wines tend to be acidic. White wines that are very acidic taste crisp and/or tart whereas less acidic ones are flabby or flat.
